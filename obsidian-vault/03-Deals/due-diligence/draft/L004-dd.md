## Top 5 Facts That Change the Buyer's Read

1. **The SDE math does not add up at face value.** $294k net on $603k revenue with 4 employees implies owner is working full-time and the "cash flow" includes substantial owner compensation addback. The true absentee CF (after replacing the owner's labor with a manager) could be $100k–$180k — which is a very different deal.

2. **The asking price signals something.** Selling a $295k/yr CF business for $315k (1.07x multiple) is below any rational seller calculus unless the seller has urgency (health, partnership dispute, leases expiring) or there are undisclosed liabilities. This requires direct explanation from the seller.

3. **Gasket Guy is in the same market.** A national franchise (gasketguy.com) operates in Savannah. If the listed business is NOT a Gasket Guy franchise, then it's competing against a brand that has national accounts and preferred vendor contracts with restaurant chains. Account vulnerability is real.

4. **Geography is borderline.** Savannah is 3.5–4 hours from Atlanta. This is not a semi-absentee business a first-time buyer can manage with 12 hrs/wk from Atlanta. It requires either (a) a strong existing manager or (b) buyer willingness to be more hands-on remotely, which conflicts with the buyer's day-job constraint.

5. **B2B recurring route with low capital requirements.** This is the right business model for the buyer — no inventory, low overhead, recurring service contract revenue, scalable via route density. If the financials are real and the operational model is what it appears, this is the most unusual value proposition in this discovery set.

## Recommended Next Step

**Conditional interest** — contact broker to understand the price anomaly before investing further diligence time. If seller explains the 1x multiple with a credible non-catastrophic reason (lease expiration, health, partnership buyout), escalate to NDA + full CIM.
