## Top 5 Facts That Change the Buyer's Read

1. **The adjusted EBITDA is misleading.** The $174k figure requires eliminating the ops manager — but if the buyer cannot be on-site daily, the ops manager is non-negotiable. True semi-absentee CF is $107k, below target.

2. **Owner is not semi-absentee in the meaningful sense.** 12-15 hrs/wk is real involvement, but it's entirely in revenue-generating activities (sales). Any buyer who doesn't replace that function will see revenue decline. This is a business that runs operationally without the owner, but not commercially.

3. **The franchise is an asset and a liability simultaneously.** A known, reputable cleaning franchise brand brings training, processes, and client credibility — but it also means ongoing royalties, franchisor oversight, and transfer approval risk. Knowing the franchise brand is a prerequisite to any decision.

4. **62 clients at $9.6k average = reasonable concentration.** At $597k revenue / 62 clients, no single client should exceed $100k without being disclosed. This concentration profile is acceptable if top-5 clients account for <30% of revenue.

5. **13 years in business is meaningful.** Established 2012 — the operational model has survived post-COVID cleaning market upheaval. This is evidence of a durable business, not a pre-revenue story.

## Recommended Next Step

**Request NDA and ask broker for franchise name.** If franchise is a top-tier brand (e.g., Coverall, Stratus, Jani-King, Vanguard) with a reasonable transfer process, this deal improves significantly. CF gap to $140k target requires negotiation or price reduction to ~$300k.
